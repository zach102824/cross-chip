VQE-RELATED PAPERS — DISCUSSION SUMMARY
========================================

1. Variational Quantum Eigensolver with Reduced Circuit Complexity (ClusterVQE)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Authors: Yu Zhang, Lukasz Cincio, Christian F. A. Negre, Piotr Czarnik,
         Patrick J. Coles, Petr M. Anisimov, Susan M. Mniszewski,
         Sergei Tretiak, Pavel A. Dub
Journal: npj Quantum Information, vol. 8, Article 96 (2022)
DOI: 10.1038/s41534-022-00599-z
Link: https://www.nature.com/articles/s41534-022-00599-z

Summary:
Introduces ClusterVQE, an algorithm that reduces both circuit depth and
qubit count (width) in VQE for quantum chemistry. Qubits are split into
clusters based on mutual information (MI), a measure of correlation
between spin-orbitals — qubits that are strongly correlated are grouped
into the same cluster, using classical graph-partitioning (e.g. Metis)
or QUBO/quantum-annealer methods. Entanglement WITHIN a cluster is built
normally via entangling gates on a quantum circuit. Entanglement BETWEEN
clusters is not built on hardware at all — instead it is folded
classically into a "dressed" Hamiltonian, so each cluster can be
measured on its own smaller, shallower circuit. The ansatz is grown
adaptively (ADAPT-style), and operator selection at each iteration uses
gradient measurements. Clustering itself can be done from a fixed
classical MI (via MP2/DMRG) or updated on-the-fly during optimization.
Demonstrated on LiH, (H2)2, N2, and on real IBM quantum hardware
(IBMQ-Bogota) with error mitigation (Clifford data regression).

Key takeaway: reduces quantum hardware requirements (fewer qubits,
shallower circuits) at the cost of additional classical computation,
by removing inter-cluster entanglement via Hamiltonian dressing rather
than by qubit rearrangement.


2. Correlation-Informed Permutation of Qubits for Reducing Ansatz Depth
   in the Variational Quantum Eigensolver (PermVQE)
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
Authors: Nikolay V. Tkachenko, James Sud, Yu Zhang, Sergei Tretiak,
         Petr M. Anisimov, Andrew T. Arrasmith, Patrick J. Coles,
         Lukasz Cincio, Pavel A. Dub
Journal: PRX Quantum, vol. 2, 020337 (2021)
DOI: 10.1103/PRXQuantum.2.020337
arXiv: 2009.04996

Summary:
Proposes PermVQE, which adds an extra optimization loop to standard VQE
that permutes (reassigns) which physical qubit each spin-orbital maps
to, rather than removing or restructuring entanglement. The permutation
is chosen — again based on the mutual information between spin-orbitals
— to place strongly correlated qubits physically adjacent to one
another on the chip's actual connectivity graph (tested on linear and
2D-grid architectures). This minimizes the number of SWAP gates /
long-range CNOTs needed to implement entangling operations, directly
addressing hardware topology constraints. The approach is ansatz-
agnostic: demonstrated with standard VQE and also with a fermionic-
ADAPT-derived pseudo-Trotter ansatz, achieving nearly a 2x reduction in
CNOT count on a linear-connectivity architecture. Tested on LiH, H2,
(H2)2, H4, H3+, and N2.

Key takeaway: rather than eliminating inter-qubit entanglement (as in
ClusterVQE), PermVQE keeps all qubits on one circuit but optimizes their
physical layout against a given hardware coupling map, reducing SWAP
overhead for topology-constrained devices.


HOW THE TWO RELATE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
- ClusterVQE: splits the problem across multiple smaller circuits by
  removing inter-cluster entanglement (via a dressed Hamiltonian).
  Reduces qubit count per circuit.
- PermVQE: keeps one circuit but reorders qubit-to-orbital assignment
  to match hardware topology, minimizing SWAP gates. Reduces circuit
  depth via layout optimization, not via removing entanglement.
- Both use the same underlying tool — mutual information between
  spin-orbitals/qubits, computable classically via MP2 or small-bond-
  dimension DMRG — as the correlation metric that drives their
  respective optimizations (clustering vs. permutation).
- ClusterVQE builds on / cites PermVQE as prior work from the same
  research group (Los Alamos National Laboratory).
